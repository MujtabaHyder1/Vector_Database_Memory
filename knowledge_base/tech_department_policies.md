# Tech Department Policies

## Device Request Policy

Employees can request laptops, desktops, monitors, keyboards, mice, headsets, docking stations, phones, and replacement chargers through the tech department.

All device requests must include the employee name, department, manager name, business reason, requested device type, preferred date, and whether the device is for office, remote, or hybrid work.

Standard laptop requests are fulfilled within 5 business days after manager approval. Urgent laptop requests require department head approval and must include a clear business impact.

The standard laptop budget is 2000 USD. Requests above 2000 USD require finance and department head approval.

New hires should receive their approved device no later than their start date when the onboarding request is submitted at least 7 business days in advance.

Device refresh is available every 4 years, or earlier if the device has a verified performance, reliability, security, or compatibility issue.

Contractors may receive company devices only when the contract owner confirms the assignment duration, access requirements, and return date.

## Device Issue and Support Policy

Employees should report device issues as soon as possible. The request must include the device type, asset tag if available, operating system, issue summary, error messages, urgency, and troubleshooting already attempted.

Critical issues include device failure, security incidents, lost or stolen devices, inability to work, executive support blockers, and production access blockers. Critical issues target first response within 1 business hour.

High priority issues include repeated crashes, VPN failure, broken camera or microphone before a scheduled meeting, and blocked access to required business apps. High priority issues target first response within 4 business hours.

Normal issues target first response within 1 business day.

Lost or stolen devices must be reported immediately. The tech department will remotely lock or wipe managed devices where possible and open a security review.

Employees must not repair company devices through unapproved third-party repair shops. Hardware repair must be arranged by the tech department.

## Access and Software Policy

Software installation requests must include the tool name, vendor, business purpose, license need, data sensitivity, and manager approval.

Paid software requires budget owner approval before purchase.

Software that processes customer data, employee data, financial data, source code, or confidential company material requires security review before approval.

Admin access is temporary by default and must include a reason, affected system, duration, and manager approval.

Shared accounts are not allowed unless formally approved for a service account use case.

## Remote Work Equipment Policy

Remote and hybrid employees may request one monitor, keyboard, mouse, headset, webcam, and docking station when their role requires regular remote work.

Home internet reimbursement is handled by HR and Finance, not the tech department.

The tech department supports company-managed devices remotely, but employees are responsible for personal network equipment unless a company network device was issued.

## Device Return Policy

Employees must return company devices, chargers, security keys, phones, and accessories on their last working day or by the date agreed with HR.

Managers are responsible for confirming returned equipment during offboarding.

Missing or damaged equipment is reviewed by HR, Finance, and the tech department before any recovery action is taken.

## Knowledge Response Rules

When answering device request questions, mention required request details, approval requirements, budget limits, and expected timeline when relevant.

When answering device issue questions, ask for missing troubleshooting details and classify urgency using critical, high, or normal priority.

When answering access or software questions, mention manager approval, budget approval for paid tools, and security review for sensitive data.

When the policy does not contain enough information, say what is known and ask for the missing details instead of inventing a rule.
